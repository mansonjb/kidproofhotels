Ask any parent what they want from a hotel and, before the pool or the location, they will name the same thing: a room the whole family actually fits in. The short answer is that you should never trust a "sleeps 4" label on its own. Ask the hotel exactly what the beds are, whether a connecting door is guaranteed at booking, and whether a cot fits once the beds are in. Get those three answers and the rest of the holiday falls into place.

## What is the difference between connecting rooms, a family room and a suite?

The words hotels use are slippery, so learn them before you pay:

- **Family room:** one big room that officially sleeps four or more. Great for young kids, tight once they are older.
- **Connecting rooms:** two separate rooms with a private door between them. The gold standard with a baby who naps, or teens who want their own space and their own bathroom.
- **Family suite:** a room with a separate sleeping area, sometimes a small lounge. The comfortable middle ground.
- **Adjoining rooms:** next door to each other but with no connecting door. Not the same thing, and an easy, expensive mistake to make.

If a listing says "sleeps 4" but the photo shows one double bed, the other two beds are almost always a sofa bed or a pull-out. Always check what the extra beds actually are before you assume everyone gets a proper mattress.

## Which setup suits which age?

The right shape changes as the children grow:

- **Baby and toddler:** a connecting room or a separate sleeping area is worth paying for. It means bedtime at 7pm does not plunge the whole family into darkness, and you can sit up next door with the monitor on.
- **Young kids (roughly 4 to 9):** a family room usually works fine. They are happy sharing, and you all still want to be in earshot.
- **Tweens and teens:** connecting rooms come back into their own. A door they can close, ideally a second bathroom, and the peace that comes with both.

## What should I ask the hotel before booking?

A quick email to the hotel is worth ten reviews. Get five answers in writing:

1. How many the room really sleeps, and what the actual beds are (double, twins, sofa bed).
2. Whether the extra bed is a proper single or a fold-out sofa.
3. Whether connecting rooms are available and **guaranteed at booking**, not just "on request".
4. Whether a cot is free of charge and still fits once the beds are set up.
5. Whether the rooms can be made up and ready before you arrive.

"On request" is the phrase to watch. It means the hotel will try, not that it is confirmed, and with two connecting rooms you do not want to gamble on arrival.

## Connecting rooms or a family room: which is better value?

It depends on the ages and your budget. A family room is almost always cheaper and is plenty for younger children. Two connecting rooms cost more, sometimes close to double, but buy you separate sleep and separate space, which matters more every year the kids get older.

| | Family room | Connecting rooms |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lower, one room | Higher, often near double |
| Best for | Babies to young kids | Toddlers who nap, tweens, teens |
| Bedtime | Everyone shares the dark | Adults stay up next door |
| Space | One bathroom, shared | Two rooms, often two bathrooms |

Across the Mediterranean and the Canaries, the resorts that take families seriously (in Greece, Spain, Italy and the islands) tend to offer real connecting rooms and genuine family suites rather than a double with a squeezed-in sofa bed. Those are the ones worth booking early, because the family configurations sell out first.

## FAQ

**What does "connecting rooms" actually mean?**
Two separate hotel rooms joined by a private internal door, so you can move between them without going into the corridor. Adjoining rooms are simply next to each other with no shared door.

**Are connecting rooms guaranteed when I book?**
Only if the hotel says so in writing. Many list them "on request", which is not a guarantee. Ask for written confirmation before you pay.

**Is a sofa bed comfortable enough for a child?**
For a night or two, usually fine. For a full week, a proper single or twin is far better for sleep, especially for an older child or teen.

**Do connecting rooms cost more than a family room?**
Yes, usually noticeably more, because you are paying for two rooms. You get separate sleep and, often, a second bathroom in return.

**We have a baby and a toddler. What should we book?**
A connecting room or a family suite with a separate sleeping area, so the early bedtime does not black out the whole family. Confirm a free cot fits once the other beds are in.
