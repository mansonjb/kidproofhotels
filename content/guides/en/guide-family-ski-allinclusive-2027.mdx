All-inclusive skiing exists to kill the one thing that makes family ski holidays exhausting: the logistics. Instead of separately booking ski school, hiring gear, buying lift passes, arranging childcare and sorting lunch on the mountain, you pay one price and it is all handled. In the French Alps the clearest example is Club Med, whose family villages bundle children's ESF lessons, a free Mini Club, every meal (wine included) and, in most cases, ski-in ski-out access into a single booking.

## What does all-inclusive skiing actually include?

At a Club Med family village the price typically covers children's group ski lessons with the ESF, lift passes, all meals and drinks (wine and beer at lunch and dinner), the included Mini Club for 4 to 10 year olds while you ski, evening entertainment and an indoor pool to thaw everyone out. You drop the bags and the week runs itself. That is the appeal: the mountain holiday without the second job of organising it.

## Does all-inclusive skiing work with a baby or toddler?

Yes, and this is where it earns its keep. The six French Alps villages here run a Baby Club Med (from 4 months) and a Petit Club Med (2 to 3 years) at extra cost, alongside the included Mini Club (4 to 10). That means even with a baby in tow, both parents can genuinely ski, knowing exactly who has whom. Older kids and teens get their own ski groups and, at several villages, a teen space. It is one of the few formats that works from 4 months to 17 in the same booking.

## Which Club Med village suits which family?

The choice comes down to the ski area and the vibe.

| Village | Best for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Les Arcs Panorama, Peisey-Vallandry | big-area skiers, mixed abilities | Paradiski, ski-in ski-out |
| Tignes | reliable snow, keen skiers | high altitude, big terrain |
| La Rosiere | first-timers, sun | gentle beginnings, sunny slopes |
| Alpe d'Huez | sun, variety | big south-facing area |
| Grand Massif Samoens Morillon | village feel, closer to Geneva | good for younger families |

La Plagne, Les Arcs and Tignes suit families chasing a big linked area. Alpe d'Huez and La Rosiere bring more sun and gentler beginner terrain, ideal for a first family ski trip. Samoens plays the village card and sits closest to Geneva airport, handy with young children.

## What budget should you plan for?

Ski all-inclusive is not cheap, but the single price is more honest than it first looks: lessons, lift passes, meals and childcare would each be a separate line elsewhere. For the French school-holiday weeks expect a premium band per person; shoulder weeks (early December, mid-January, mid-March) fall into a more moderate band for the same village. When you add up ESF lessons plus six lift passes plus a week of mountain lunches for a family of four, the bundled price usually compares well.

## When should you book ski for 2027?

Very early. The 2027 school-holiday weeks (February and the Easter break) go first, as ski weeks always do, and family rooms in ski-in ski-out villages are the first to disappear. Book a refundable rate now and compare the villages on the map, then confirm once your dates are locked. Waiting rarely pays for peak-week family ski.

## FAQ

**Are children's ski lessons included?**
Yes. Group ESF lessons for children are part of the Club Med package at these villages, along with lift passes.

**What is the youngest age for childcare?**
The Baby Club Med takes children from 4 months (at extra cost), the Petit Club Med covers 2 to 3 year olds, and the Mini Club for 4 to 10 is included.

**Is equipment hire included?**
Ski and boot hire is usually an add-on, not part of the core price. Confirm at the village, as it can often be pre-booked.

**Are the villages ski-in ski-out?**
Most are, which matters a lot with children and kit to carry. Check each village, since a couple sit a short shuttle from the lifts.

**Which village is best for beginners?**
La Rosiere and Alpe d'Huez have the sunniest, gentlest beginner terrain, making them the easiest choice for a first family ski week.
