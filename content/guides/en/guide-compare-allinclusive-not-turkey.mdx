Yes, you can match an Antalya all-inclusive without leaving the EU, and you give up almost nothing. Greece, Spain and Italy now run family resorts that rival anything in Turkey: proper kids clubs, water parks, generous board and, in the best cases, a la carte dining included. What you gain by staying inside the EU is a shorter flight from western Europe, no currency to change, familiar consumer rules and, for many families, simple peace of mind. Here is how the three countries compare and where each one wins.

## Why choose Europe over Turkey for all-inclusive?

Antalya is genuinely excellent value and no one sensible disputes that. But plenty of families prefer to stay inside the bloc: flights are often shorter, you keep the euro, EHIC/GHIC-style healthcare cover and EU air-passenger rights apply, and there is no visa admin. Add a personal pull towards Greek islands or Spanish costas and the choice makes itself. The point of this guide is that you sacrifice neither the water park nor the all-in comfort by doing so.

## Which country does all-inclusive best?

Greece has caught up and, at the top end, overtaken. The Ikos model on Corfu, Kos and Halkidiki brought a la carte restaurants, premium drinks and slick kids clubs into the all-inclusive price, and resorts like Sani on Halkidiki set the standard for family infrastructure. Greece is the pick when you want the flagship all-inclusive experience.

## When does Spain win?

Spain wins on consistency and flight time. From the Canaries (Tenerife, Gran Canaria) to the mainland costas and the Balearics (Mallorca), the resorts are well drilled, the season is long and, crucially, the Canaries deliver all-inclusive sun in winter when Greece is cold. If you are travelling out of summer, or you want the shortest possible hop to a reliable resort, Spain is the safe answer.

## Where does Italy fit in?

Italy is the wildcard, and Puglia is its family heartland: big beach resorts along the Ionian coast, strong on food and on that unhurried southern-Italian feel. All-inclusive is less ubiquitous than in Greece or Spain, but the resorts that do it, do it with real character and excellent cooking. Choose Italy when the holiday is as much about the place and the plate as the pool.

## What board and budget should a family expect?

Terms matter. "All-inclusive" ranges from basic (buffet plus local-brand drinks) to premium (a la carte restaurants, branded drinks, sometimes activities), so read what is bundled before you compare prices. Expect mid-market family all-inclusive in Greece and Spain to be strong value in the shoulder months of May, June, September and October, with July and August the peak. The Ikos-tier premium resorts sit at the high end, but often work out cheaper than they look once you count the dining and drinks you would otherwise pay for on top.

## FAQ

### Is European all-inclusive as good as Turkey's?
At the top end, yes, and arguably better: the Ikos-style Greek resorts include a la carte dining that many Turkish all-inclusives charge extra for. Turkey often wins on headline price, Europe on flight time and included quality.

### Which is best for a winter all-inclusive?
Spain's Canary Islands, without question. Greece and Italy are too cool for a beach holiday from November to March, while Tenerife and Gran Canaria run warm, all-inclusive resorts all year.

### Where do you get the best value in Europe?
Mainland Spain (the costas and Costa de Almeria) and mid-market Greece in the shoulder months. Book May, June, September or early October and you get warm sea, lower prices and thinner crowds.

### Do these resorts have water parks and kids clubs?
Yes. The resorts we score across Greece, Spain and Italy include on-site water parks, free kids clubs and family rooms, everything you would look for in Antalya, without leaving western Europe.
