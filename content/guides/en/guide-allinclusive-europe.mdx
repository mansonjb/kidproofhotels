All-inclusive has an image problem, and most of the people sneering at it never travelled with a four-year-old. With young kids it can be the quiet difference between a holiday and a week of sums, of saying no to the third ice cream because lunch is already paid for twice over. The catch is that it only works when the details are right, and two resorts at the same price can hand you two completely different weeks.

## Getting your 2027 dates before they go

The best family resorts sell their peak weeks early, and then earlier still. For July and August 2027, the names families chase, the Ikos resorts in Greece, the big Canary Island properties, start filling from autumn 2026, and the sharpest early-booking deals go before anything else. Book a refundable rate now, keep half an eye on the price, and lock it when the early-bird opens. No risk, and one less thing to worry about.

## When it actually pays for itself

The maths tips your way the moment your children want a snack every hour: the free ice creams and drinks add up fast. Same if you would rather not go hunting for a kid-friendly restaurant twice a day, or if a club and a couple of pools are enough to fill the time. It pays off less if your kids are older and itching to explore, or if the food is the whole point of the trip. Worth knowing before you pay.

## What separates the gem from the grind

This is where the week is won or lost. A decent buffet with an early sitting, because children eat at six, not eight. A kids' counter at their height, stocked with food they will actually touch. Snacks and drinks all day, not just at mealtimes. A la carte restaurants included, rather than billed as a surprise once you arrive. And above all a room that fits: the finest buffet on the coast cannot fix four people crammed into one. Connecting rooms and real family suites earn their premium.

> The best family all-inclusives are not the ones with the most bars. They are the ones that quietly solve the logistics of feeding and entertaining children all day, so you stop thinking about it.

## Greece, Spain, Italy

Three countries do family all-inclusive better than anywhere else. Greece sets the pace, the Ikos resorts especially, with included a la carte dining, proper kids clubs and beaches built for the paddling age. Spain brings the consistency: the Canaries deliver warm sea and sun in every month of the year, while the Almeria coast pairs big resorts with quieter sand. Italy rounds it off, Puglia offering a calmer, more food-led take on the family week.
