Rhodes is one of the most reassuring Greek islands to book for a family: the southeast coast is a run of calm, sandy, gently shelving beaches lined with big all-inclusive resorts, the medieval old town rescues any rainy afternoon, and the season is so long you can still swim over October half-term. The flight from western Europe is around three and a half to four hours. For 2027, base yourself between Kiotari and Faliraki, book a refundable rate early, and you have a week that works from toddlers to teenagers.

## Where on Rhodes should families stay?

The best family addresses sit on the calmer southeast coast. Around Kiotari and Lardos the sea is gentle, the sand is real, and the big water-park resorts compete for your booking. Kolymbia and Kallithea, a little further north, are handy for the old town while staying quiet. Faliraki suits families who like some buzz, a water park and plenty of restaurants within reach. Lindos you earn: gorgeous setting and a famous acropolis, but steep lanes, cobbles and fewer big-resort facilities, so it is better as a day trip or for families with older kids. The windy west coast around Ixia is for kite and windsurfers more than paddling toddlers.

## When is the best time to go?

Rhodes has one of the longest beach seasons in Greece. July and August are hot and dry, often 31 to 35C, with a warm sea and big crowds. June and September are the family sweet spot: 27 to 30C, calmer resorts, and softer prices. What sets Rhodes apart is the tail of the season: October stays mild, around 23 to 25C by day, and the sea, warmed all summer, is still swimmable through late October, which makes it a strong pick for the autumn half-term when much of the Med has cooled.

## What will a week cost?

Rhodes covers every budget. At the affordable end are simpler beach hotels and self-catering studios, often near a village and very family-friendly. Mid-range buys a proper family room, a couple of pools, a kids club and half-board or all-inclusive. The high end is the polished all-inclusive with several restaurants and a large water park. All-inclusive genuinely pays off here at the constant-snacks age and because many resorts sit a little apart from restaurants; with teenagers who want to explore, half-board plus the odd taverna trip can give better food and value.

## What is there to do beyond the beach?

Plenty, which is why Rhodes suits mixed-age families. Rhodes Old Town is a walled medieval maze, one of the best-preserved in Europe, and genuinely fun with kids: the Palace of the Grand Master, the Street of the Knights, ice cream in the shade. Lindos has its acropolis above whitewashed lanes. Add the Valley of the Butterflies, the beaches at Prasonisi where two seas meet, boat trips and a couple of water parks, and you can fill a week even if the weather turns.

## Rhodes or Crete for a first family trip?

Both are excellent and easy. Rhodes wins on the length of its swimming season and on rainy-day charm thanks to the old town, and its southeast resorts are compact and calm. Crete wins on sheer variety, food and the range of beaches, though transfers can be longer. For a straightforward, long beach week with a historic town on the doorstep, Rhodes is hard to beat; for a more varied island with more to explore, lean Crete.

## FAQ

**Is Rhodes good for toddlers?** Yes. The southeast coast around Kiotari and Lardos has calm, gently shelving sand. Choose a resort with a shallow pool and a real family room, and avoid the windy west coast.

**How long is the flight from western Europe?** Around three and a half to four hours to Rhodes from the UK, France, Germany and the Benelux, direct in summer.

**Can we still swim in October?** Yes, that is Rhodes's edge. The sea stays swimmable through late October, around 23 to 24C, which makes it a strong autumn half-term choice.

**Is Lindos a good base with young children?** Not ideal. It is beautiful but hilly and cobbled with fewer big-resort facilities. Base near Kiotari or Faliraki and visit Lindos for the day.

**Is all-inclusive worth it in Rhodes?** With young children, usually yes, as many resorts sit apart from restaurants. With teenagers keen to explore, half-board plus taverna trips can work out better.
