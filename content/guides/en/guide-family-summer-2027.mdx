For peak summer 2027, the Mediterranean is the safest bet for a family: warm sea, long evenings, and resorts running at full tilt. Our shortlist leans on Greece, with strong picks in Spain and Italy, and the honest advice is to choose the coast around your children's ages, then book early, because July and August sell out first and the soft prices go with them.

## Where should we go for summer sun with kids?

Summer is the one season where almost the whole Mediterranean works, so the choice is about fit, not weather. Greece leads our scored family resorts: Halkidiki and Corfu on the mainland side, the islands of Kos and Zakynthos for classic beach weeks. Spain covers the Balearics (Menorca, Ibiza, Mallorca) with short flights and calm bays, plus the Costa Dorada for big pools and water parks. Italy's Puglia adds something different: a real region to explore, masserie with pools, and food the whole table actually wants.

Flights from western Europe are short by summer-holiday standards: roughly 2 to 3 hours to the Balearics and Puglia, and around 3 to 3.5 hours to Greece. Nothing here needs a long-haul day, which matters with a toddler.

## Which resort suits our children's ages?

Match the destination to the kids and the week gets easier.

- **Babies and toddlers (0 to 4):** shallow, gently shelving beaches and a paddling pool close to the room. Menorca's south-coast bays, calmer Greek resorts and Puglia's protected coves are kind at this age.
- **Kids (5 to 11):** this is the water-slide age. Big pool complexes and a genuine all-day kids club earn their keep. The Costa Dorada and larger island resorts shine.
- **Teens:** they want a pool scene, watersports and something to do after dinner. Livelier resorts and those near a town suit them best.

## What does a great summer family resort actually need?

A free kids club that runs all day, not a token hour, because this is the week you dream of two hours to yourself. Pools built for the water-slide age, plus a gentle area for the little ones. An early dinner with food children will eat, so evenings stay easy in the heat. Air conditioning that works in the sleeping area, and rooms that fit, ideally with a separate sleeping space or a connecting door.

## All-inclusive or half-board in summer?

In peak heat, all-inclusive earns its place: nobody wants to trek out at midday, and snacks, ice creams and drinks by the pool add up fast otherwise. It suits younger families and anyone who wants the budget fixed before they fly. Half-board keeps the evenings free for a village taverna and works well in Puglia or Menorca, where eating out is part of the holiday. As a rough guide, budget resorts sit at the lower per-night bands, mid-range family clubs in the middle, and the polished all-inclusive names at the top.

## When should we book summer 2027?

Early. Summer is the week nobody can move around, so the best family rooms and the softer prices go first, often by late winter. The sensible play is to hold a refundable room now and keep comparing, rather than wait for a deal that rarely comes in July and August.

## FAQ

**When is the sea warmest in the Med?** August into early September, when the water sits around 25 to 27C across Greece, Spain and Italy: the most reliable stretch for nervous swimmers.

**Is August too hot for a baby?** It can be. Choose a resort with shade, a pool near the room and reliable air conditioning, keep the middle of the day quiet, and the Balearics or northern Greece over the hottest southern spots.

**How far ahead should we book for July or August?** Aim for the winter before. Peak-summer family rooms are the first to sell, and prices only climb closer to the dates.

**Which is cheapest?** Costa Dorada and mainland Greek all-inclusives tend to offer the best value per family; polished island resorts and Puglia sit higher.

**Do we need a car?** Not at a self-contained beach resort. In Puglia or rural Menorca a car turns the week into a proper explore, so it is worth it there.
