A free kids club that runs all day and is split by age is one of the most valuable things a family hotel can offer, and it is also the feature most likely to be oversold. "Kids club" can mean a genuine, staffed, morning-to-evening club that hands you a real break, or a room with a few crayons that opens for two hours if enough children turn up. Some clubs are included in the price, others quietly cost 50 to 100 euros a day per child, which changes the maths of a whole holiday. Here is how to tell them apart before you book.

## Is the club actually free, or is it extra?

This is the first question, and the one hotels are vaguest about. A resort that looks cheaper on the nightly rate can end up dearer once you add a paid club for two children across a week. Read the small print: "kids club available" often means available at a cost. When we score a hotel, a genuinely free, included club scores far higher than a paid one, because that is how a family actually experiences it. Across the Canaries (Tenerife, Gran Canaria, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura), the Greek islands (Kos, Corfu, Crete, Rhodes) and the Balearics (Majorca, Ibiza, Menorca), the strongest family resorts fold a real club into the price rather than selling it back to you.

## How do I tell a real club from a token one?

The problem is that a proper club and a babysitting corner describe themselves the same way online. These questions separate them:

- **What hours does it run?** All day (roughly 9am to 6pm) is a real club. Two hours after lunch is a holding pen.
- **Is it split by age?** Good clubs separate toddlers (roughly 3 to 5) from older children (6 to 12), with a teen zone on top. One room for ages 3 to 12 suits nobody.
- **What is the staff-to-child ratio?** A vague answer is a bad sign. Younger groups need closer supervision.
- **Do you have to book, and is there a summer waiting list?** Popular free clubs fill fast in July and August.
- **Is there an evening session?** A mini-disco or evening club is what buys the grown-ups a quiet dinner.

## What age does a kids club actually suit?

Match the club to your child, because a brilliant club is wasted on a baby who cannot use it, and a toddler club bores a ten year old. Most clubs start at potty-trained age (around 3 or 4); under that you are looking at a paid creche or a babysitter, not a club. From 4 to 8 is the sweet spot, when children throw themselves into the crafts, treasure hunts and pool games. Teenagers need a separate teen club with sport, games and their own space, or they will refuse outright. | | Free included club | Paid club (50-100 euros/day) |

| Cost for 2 kids, 7 days | Nothing extra | Roughly 700 to 1,400 euros |

For most families a free, full-day, age-split club is worth more than a spa, a sea view or a second pool. It is the single feature that turns a holiday with children into a holiday.

## FAQ

**Are kids clubs usually free?**
It varies. At many all-inclusive family resorts in Greece, Spain and Croatia the daytime club is included, while premium creche care for under-3s and some evening childcare cost extra. Always confirm before booking.

**What age do kids clubs start?**
Most begin at 3 or 4, once a child is out of nappies. Younger children are handled by a separate (usually paid) baby club or creche, or by a babysitter.

**Do I need to book the kids club in advance?**
For popular free clubs in peak summer, yes. Ask the hotel whether places are capped and whether there is a waiting list in July and August.

**What is a good staff-to-child ratio?**
Lower is better, especially for toddlers. If a hotel cannot give you a figure, treat it as a red flag rather than a detail.
