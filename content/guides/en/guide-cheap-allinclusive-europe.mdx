Cheap all-inclusive has a reputation to shake off: the sad buffet, the crowded pool. It is not always deserved. The best value in Europe sits on the Spanish costas, the Canaries and the Greek islands, where deep supply keeps prices honest, with Turkey often undercutting the lot if a slightly longer flight suits you. The trick is telling the resorts that nail the basics from the ones that cut exactly the corner that ruins the week.

## What should cheap all-inclusive still include?

The first things to vanish at the bottom of the market are the free kids club and a genuinely useful pool, so those are the two to confirm first. Then check the buffet serves early enough for young children and offers more than chips, so you are not topping up at the local shop every evening. Make sure family rooms sleep four without turning bedtime into Tetris. And read the drinks policy closely: real all-inclusive means local drinks and snacks genuinely included through the day, not a list of three things with everything else charged extra.

## Where is all-inclusive cheapest for families in Europe?

Value tracks supply. The Spanish costas (Costa Dorada, Costa del Sol, Costa de Almeria) and the Balearics (Ibiza, Majorca, Menorca) have huge inventory and short flights of around two to two and a half hours. The Canaries (Gran Canaria, Tenerife, Fuerteventura) cost a little more to reach, four to four and a half hours, but deliver reliable sun year round. The Greek islands (Corfu, Crete, Rhodes, Kos, Zakynthos) are strong value in the May to October season. Turkey's Aegean and Mediterranean coasts, around Bodrum and Antalya, are frequently the cheapest of all for the standard you get, with flights of three and a half to four hours.

## How much should a budget family week cost?

Think in bands rather than exact figures, since prices swing with dates. Genuine budget all-inclusive lands at the lower end of the market, typically a modest per-night rate for a family room; mid-range buys you a better club, more restaurants and a nicer pool complex; high-end is a different category altogether. The single biggest lever is timing: the same room in late August can cost roughly double the same week in June or September. Travelling in the shoulder months, or outside the school holidays where you can, is the surest way to keep a family week affordable.

## What corners get cut at the cheap end, and which matter?

Some cuts are fine, others ruin the week. You can usually live without à la carte restaurants, premium spirits, a spa or a huge room. What you cannot skip: a free, staffed kids club if you were relying on it; a pool big enough for the crowd; buffet hours that suit small children; and air conditioning that actually works in July. Before booking, check recent family reviews specifically for buffet variety, pool crowding and club availability, the three things budget resorts most often skimp on.

## Is all-inclusive actually cheaper than half-board?

For families with children who snack constantly and drink through a hot day, all-inclusive usually wins on both cost and stress, because it removes the running tab and the mental maths at every meal. Half-board (breakfast and dinner) can be cheaper on paper and works if you like to explore and eat out at lunch, but you will pay for every drink, ice cream and poolside snack. The busier your children and the hotter the resort, the more all-inclusive pays off.

## FAQ

**Does cheap all-inclusive mean bad food?**
Not necessarily. Budget resorts run buffets rather than à la carte, but many do the basics well. Check recent reviews for buffet variety and serving hours before you book.

**When should I book a budget family week for 2027?**
Early. The cheapest weeks are cheap because families snapped them up in good time, so book refundable as soon as dates are set, ideally by late winter for summer.

**Are drinks really included at budget resorts?**
Local drinks usually are; imported brands and premium spirits often are not. Read the all-inclusive policy line by line so there are no surprises poolside.

**How do I avoid the crowded-pool problem?**
Favour resorts with more than one pool, or a separate splash area for children, and travel in the shoulder months when occupancy drops but the weather still holds.
