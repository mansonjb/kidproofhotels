Once November arrives, guaranteed winter sun for a European family really comes down to three options: the Canaries, Cape Verde and the Red Sea. The short answer: the Canaries are the simplest and shortest trip, the Red Sea gives the most dependable heat and the best snorkelling, and Cape Verde is the quiet middle ground. They differ on temperature, flight length and budget, so the best pick depends on your children's ages and your appetite for travel.

## Where can a family find guaranteed sun after November?

These three regions are the ones that stay reliably warm through the European winter. The Canaries (Tenerife, Gran Canaria, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura) sit off West Africa and hold 20 to 24 degrees. Cape Verde, further south in the Atlantic, runs a touch warmer at 25 to 27 degrees. The Red Sea coast of Egypt (Sharm el-Sheikh, Hurghada) is the hot-weather champion in deep winter, often 22 to 26 degrees on land with warm, calm, crystal-clear water. Elsewhere in the wider region you will also see families choosing the Gulf (Dubai, Ras al-Khaimah) for a pricier, glossier version of the same guaranteed heat.

## Canaries, Cape Verde or Red Sea?

The Canaries are the gateway. A flight of roughly four to four and a half hours, EU standards, no visa and no jet lag make them the easiest choice, especially with a baby or toddler. Cape Verde (mainly the island of Sal) turns the heat up a notch and feels calmer and more remote, with lots of all-inclusive resorts, but the flight is longer at around six hours and some beaches have strong wind and currents to respect. The Red Sea delivers the most reliable warmth and, crucially for children, spectacular coral reefs in shallow water: a snorkel and a pair of goggles turn the house reef into an aquarium. Flights run about five hours and all-inclusive prices are very competitive.

## Which is best by children's age?

For a baby or toddler, the Canaries win on sheer simplicity: short flight, no jet lag, easy self-catering and familiar food. For primary-age kids who can snorkel and love a house reef, the Red Sea is hard to beat and often the cheapest all-inclusive of the three. Cape Verde suits families who want warmth and calm and do not mind a longer flight, though the wind on some beaches means it is better for confident swimmers than for wobbly first paddles. Teens will be happy anywhere with a good pool and watersports.

## What should we watch out for?

Be honest about the trade-offs. Canary sea temperatures are cool in winter, so heated pools matter. Cape Verde can be windy, and currents on the open Atlantic beaches deserve respect with young children. On the Red Sea, take the usual care with food and water hygiene at all-inclusive buffets, use good reef-safe sun protection, and remember that the desert nights cool down. Everywhere, the Christmas, February and Easter school holidays are the peak, so the best family resorts book up months ahead.

## FAQ

**Which winter-sun region is warmest?**
Cape Verde is the warmest in the air, around 25 to 27 degrees, with the Red Sea close behind and offering the calmest, clearest water.

**Which has the shortest flight?**
The Canaries, at roughly four to four and a half hours from western Europe, versus about five for the Red Sea and six for Cape Verde.

**Which is best value?**
The Red Sea usually offers the most all-inclusive for your money, followed closely by Cape Verde.

**Is the Red Sea good for young children?**
Yes, especially for snorkelling: the coral reefs sit in shallow, calm water, which is ideal for children just starting out with a mask.

**Do we need a visa or vaccinations?**
The Canaries need neither for EU families. Cape Verde and Egypt have entry requirements that change, so always check the current rules for your nationality before booking.
