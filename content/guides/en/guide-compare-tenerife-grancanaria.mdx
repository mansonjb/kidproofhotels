For a family week of guaranteed winter sun with a short-ish flight, both islands work. The quick answer: pick Tenerife for the biggest resorts, the widest choice of restaurants and the comfort of a polished south coast; pick Gran Canaria for better value, more self-catering and a slightly gentler, more varied feel. Neither is a wrong choice, so the real question is what your particular family wants from the week.

## What do the two islands actually share?

Both sit off the coast of West Africa, so both deliver the same headline: sun almost every day of the year, daytime temperatures around 20 to 24 degrees even in December and January, and a sea that stays swimmable in winter (cool, around 19 to 20 degrees, but fine for kids who do not mind a shiver). Both have a purpose-built resort south, big water parks, long southern beaches and a flight of roughly four to four and a half hours from western Europe. Both are Spanish, so no currency faff, EU healthcare cover, familiar supermarkets and food that toddlers will eat.

## Which island suits which family?

Tenerife is the comfort island. The south (Costa Adeje, Los Cristianos, Playa de las Americas) has the densest cluster of large four and five star resorts, the best kids clubs, the most restaurant choice for a fussy eater, and the headline day out: Mount Teide, Spain's highest peak, with a cable car that older kids love. It runs a little pricier and a little livelier.

Gran Canaria plays variety and value. Maspalomas and its famous dunes, Playa del Ingles and pretty Puerto de Mogan sit alongside a big stock of aparthotels and self-catering bungalows. That kitchen of your own is a real advantage with a baby or a picky toddler, and the island often lands a notch cheaper than Tenerife for a like-for-like stay.

## Which is easier with a baby or toddler?

Gran Canaria has the edge for the youngest, mostly because self-catering is so easy to find: your own fridge, a hob for warming milk and purees, and a bit of space to nap in. Maspalomas beaches shelve gently. Tenerife wins once children are three or four and ready for a big resort kids club, a proper water park and a full all-inclusive board. Neither island is a long transfer nightmare: from either southern airport you are usually 20 to 45 minutes from your resort.

## What budget should we expect?

Both islands span the full range. A budget week means a simple aparthotel or a three star on half board. Mid-range is the sweet spot here: a solid four star resort with a kids club and good pools, on all-inclusive or half board. High-end means the marquee five stars of Costa Adeje or the big Meloneras and Maspalomas resorts. As a rule of thumb, Gran Canaria will save you a little at every tier, while Tenerife offers more at the very top end. Winter school holidays (Christmas, February half-term) are the peak, so book those early.

## FAQ

**Which island is cheaper for a family?**
Gran Canaria, usually. Self-catering aparthotels are plentiful and mid-range resorts tend to price a little below their Tenerife equivalents.

**Is the sea warm enough to swim in winter?**
It is swimmable but cool, around 19 to 20 degrees from December to March. Most kids manage; resort pools are often heated, which helps.

**How long is the flight from western Europe?**
Roughly four to four and a half hours to either island, direct from most major airports.

**When should we book for the February half-term?**
As early as you can. Winter school holidays are the busiest week of the year on both islands, and the best family resorts sell out months ahead.

**Which island is better with a toddler?**
Gran Canaria, thanks to easy self-catering and gently shelving Maspalomas beaches. Tenerife pulls ahead once children are ready for a big resort kids club.
