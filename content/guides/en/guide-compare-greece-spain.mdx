Greece or Spain for a family holiday? For a premium all-inclusive summer with the best sandy, shallow beaches, choose Greece. For year-round sun, the shortest flights and the widest range of prices, choose Spain. These are the two giants of the European family holiday: Greece has exploded in recent years and now sets the standard for luxury clubs, while Spain remains the old reliable that works in every season and on every budget. The choice mostly comes down to when you travel, how far you want to fly, and the kind of trip you are after.

## Which has the best all-inclusive?

Greece, right now, by a clear margin at the top end. The newer Greek resorts on Halkidiki, Kos, Corfu, Crete and Rhodes redefined family all-inclusive: a la carte dining included, proper kids' clubs, and long sandy beaches that shelve gently for toddlers. Spain still has enormous all-inclusive supply, and plenty of excellent family resorts, but the premium tier is more concentrated in Greece. The trade-off is distance and season, which is where Spain answers back.

## Which is better for winter and off-season sun?

Spain, without contest. The Canary Islands (Tenerife, Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, Lanzarote) sit off the African coast and stay warm all year, with winter daytime highs around 21 to 24C. That makes Spain the only mainstream European option for reliable February half-term or Christmas sun. Greece effectively closes for the beach from November to March. If you need guaranteed sun outside summer, Spain wins before the conversation starts.

## How far is the flight?

This is Spain's other big advantage for younger families. The Balearics (Majorca, Menorca, Ibiza) are roughly two hours from northern Europe, and the Costas are similar, so a flight with a baby or toddler is short and manageable. The Canaries are further, around four hours, but still year-round. Greece is generally three to four hours plus, depending on the island. For a first trip with a very young child, the shorter Spanish hop is easier on everyone.

## What about budget and season?

Spain has the deeper range, from cheap-and-cheerful to genuine luxury, so it is easier to find a bargain and easier to splurge. Greece skews mid to upper for family all-inclusive, especially at the flagship resorts in peak August. On both, June and September are the sweet spot: warm sea, thinner crowds, softer prices. Greece runs roughly April to October; Spain's mainland and Balearic season is similar, but the Canaries run all twelve months.

## FAQ

**Is Greece or Spain cheaper for families?**
Spain has the wider range and is easier for a bargain, especially outside August. Greece skews mid to upper, particularly at its newer premium resorts, though June and September soften prices on both.

**Where can we get winter sun in Europe with kids?**
The Canary Islands in Spain, which stay warm all year and are the reliable choice for February half-term or Christmas. Greece is a summer destination only.

**Which is a shorter flight with a baby?**
Spain, if you pick the Balearics or the Costas, at about two hours from northern Europe. The Canaries and most Greek islands are around four hours.

**Which has better all-inclusive?**
At the premium end, Greece leads right now, with a la carte dining and standout kids' clubs. Spain has vast all-inclusive supply and many strong family resorts across every price band.

**When is the best time to go to either?**
June and September for both: warm sea, fewer crowds and better value than the peak of August, which is hot and busy in each country.
