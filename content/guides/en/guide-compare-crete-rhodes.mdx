Crete or Rhodes? For toddlers and families who like a bit of nature and space, choose Crete. For older kids who bore quickly, want a water park and a lively resort strip, and a season that stretches into late October, choose Rhodes. Both are heavyweights of family Greece, both have sand, warm sea into autumn and big all-inclusive resorts with a proper kids' club. The difference is subtler than the brochures suggest, and it comes down to what your family actually wants from a week away.

## Which island suits toddlers and babies?

Crete has the edge with very young children. The northern beaches around Chania and Rethymno are sandy and shallow, so a two-year-old can paddle for ages without dropping off a shelf. The island is big enough that you can pick a calm western base away from the busiest strips. Rhodes works too, especially the sheltered east coast around Kiotari and Lardos, but its most famous beaches (Faliraki) skew livelier and busier. For a first holiday with a baby or toddler, Crete feels gentler and less frenetic.

## Which is better for older kids and teens?

Rhodes wins here. It is smaller and more concentrated, so everything is close: back-to-back water-park resorts, the medieval Old Town of Rhodes that genuinely rescues a rainy or too-hot afternoon, boat trips, and the Valley of the Butterflies. Older children who get restless after two days by the pool have more within easy reach. Crete rewards exploring too (Knossos, the pink sand at Elafonisi, mountain villages and gorges), but distances are real, so you trade some spontaneity for variety.

## When should we go, and how long does the season last?

Both islands run roughly late April to late October. July and August are hot and busy, with sea around 25 to 26C but daytime highs often above 32C, which is a lot for small children. June and September are the family sweet spot: sea still warm, crowds thinner, prices softer. Rhodes holds its heat a little longer, so it is the safer pick for October half-term, when you can often still swim comfortably. Crete in October is lovely but cooler and quieter, better suited to a slower trip than a beach-all-day one.

## How long is the flight and what does it cost?

From western Europe, reckon on roughly 3.5 to 4 hours to either island. Rhodes is marginally closer for most of northern Europe. Cost is broadly similar between the two, and Greece as a whole sits in the mid to upper range for family all-inclusive: think mid-range for a comfortable club resort in the shoulder season, climbing to premium in peak August or at the newer luxury properties. All-inclusive is the norm and usually the best value with kids, since it caps the daily spend.

## FAQ

**Is Crete or Rhodes better for a two-year-old?**
Crete, for its calmer, shallow northern beaches and quieter western bases. Rhodes works well too if you pick the sheltered east coast rather than the busy Faliraki strip.

**Which island is better in October?**
Rhodes. It keeps its warmth a little longer, so October half-term swimming is more reliable. Crete is still pleasant but cooler and better for a relaxed, exploring trip.

**Do we need a hire car?**
On Crete, yes if you want to explore, as distances are large. On Rhodes you can manage without one, since resorts, the Old Town and beaches sit close together.

**Which is cheaper?**
Costs are broadly similar. On both, June and September are noticeably better value than August, and all-inclusive tends to give the most predictable family budget.

**Are the resorts all-inclusive?**
Most family resorts on both islands offer all-inclusive, from solid mid-range clubs to premium properties with a la carte dining included. It is usually the best-value board type when travelling with children.
