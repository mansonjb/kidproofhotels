A few years ago you sent families to Spain without a second thought. These days Greece has taken the lead, and once you have taken children there, it is easy to see why. The sea is warm and gentle, the flights are short (roughly 3 to 4 hours from western Europe), and a handful of resorts have decided to treat families as the point rather than a complication to manage. If you want the short answer: Halkidiki and Kos are the easiest first trips, Corfu and Zakynthos are the softer, greener choice, and Crete or Rhodes suit older kids who want more to do.

## Which part of Greece is easiest with young children?

For a calm first trip, aim for Halkidiki (on the mainland, an easy transfer from Thessaloniki) or the island of Kos. Both give you big shallow bays, gentle sand and enough on site to fill the days without ever getting in a car. Corfu and Zakynthos play a greener, softer hand, lovely when you want to slow right down, though roads can be windier and transfers longer. Crete and Rhodes are bigger and busier, with more to explore, which pays off once children are past the toddler stage. The Ikos properties did a lot to reset what all-inclusive could be, with included a la carte dining and proper kids clubs, but they are not the only good option, and depending on your children they may not even be the ones you want.

## When is the best time to visit Greece with kids?

Greece has a long, reliable warm season. July and August are peak: hot (often 30 to 35 C), busy and pricey, with sea temperatures around 25 to 27 C, ideal for small children who want to stay in for hours. June and September are the sweet spot for families, still hot and sea-warm but calmer and cheaper, and school-age kids will barely notice the difference. May and October are pleasant and green with warm-enough seas in the south, better for walking and exploring than for all-day swimming. Winter is off-season on the islands, when many resorts close.

## What does a Greek family holiday cost, and is all-inclusive worth it?

Greece spans the whole range. Simpler half-board and self-catering family hotels sit at the budget end, mid-range all-inclusive resorts (the bulk of the market) land in the middle, and the flagship family resorts in Halkidiki, Corfu and Kos are firmly high-end. All-inclusive genuinely pays off with children who graze all day, and the best Greek resorts have pushed the format upmarket with a la carte restaurants and good kids' food. If you are self-catering or on half-board, budget for tavernas: Greek food is famously child-friendly, and grilled meats, bread, tzatziki and chips please almost every table.

## Islands or the mainland?

A quick comparison to steer the choice:

| | Mainland (Halkidiki) | Islands (Kos, Corfu, Rhodes, Crete) |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer from airport | Short and simple | Varies, can be long |
| Beaches | Long, shallow, sheltered bays | From calm bays to livelier resort strips |
| Feel | Green, pine-backed, low-key | More choice, more buzz, more to explore |
| Best for | Toddlers, easy first trips | Older kids, variety, water parks |

Halkidiki is the gentlest introduction. The islands give you more range once children are a bit older.

## When should I book a Greek family holiday for 2027?

Timing counts more than people expect. The best July and August 2027 weeks at the flagship family resorts start selling from autumn 2026, and the early-booking discounts leave first. The practical move is to book a refundable rate early, keep comparing, and cancel and rebook if something better appears. Waiting until spring usually means less choice and higher prices for the peak fortnight.

## FAQ

**Is Greece good for babies and toddlers?**
Yes. Shallow, sheltered bays, warm sea and short flights make it one of the easier Mediterranean options for the very young. Look for resorts with a shallow toddler pool, shade near the water and a real family room or suite.

**Which Greek island is best for a first family trip?**
Kos is a strong first-timer's island: flat, easy to get around, with long shallow beaches and lots of family resorts. On the mainland, Halkidiki is even gentler thanks to short transfers and calm bays.

**Do Greek resorts have water slides and kids clubs?**
Many do, especially the larger family resorts on Kos, Rhodes, Crete and Zakynthos. Slide parks and free kids clubs are common, though quality varies, so check ages covered and opening hours before booking.

**How long is the flight to Greece?**
Roughly 3 to 4 hours from western Europe, depending on the airport and island. Mainland Halkidiki (via Thessaloniki) and the nearer islands are at the shorter end.
